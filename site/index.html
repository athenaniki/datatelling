Did we overpay or underpay for our yard waste?

This analysis covers Orange County, North Carolina — specifically the Town of Chapel Hill's yard waste collection program. Yard waste handling costs and collection rules have both been changing lately. Below is what the Town spends, what it charges, how that compares to the recent past and to a neighboring town, and the evidence you can use to decide for yourself whether the current yard waste cart fee is reasonable, or whether you should feel grateful it isn't higher.

What's changing July 1, 2026

  • Only Town-issued carts will be accepted for trash and yard trimmings — personal cans and mis-sorted items will no longer be collected.
  • Leaves may still go in paper bags during leaf season (Nov–Jan), capped at 5 containers/week combined with yard carts.
  • Loose piles of yard trimmings, any size, cost $125/truckload — they're no longer part of free weekly collection.
  • Income-based financial assistance is available for cart purchases and other paid services, including retroactive refunds back to July 1, 2025.

Division spending over time

The Solid Waste division's total budget has risen steadily. The Town attributes this to a 0.75% retirement rate increase, a 10% jump in insurance costs, standard salary increases, and increased funding for vehicle replacement and supplies — not service expansion (staffing has stayed flat at 34.0 FTE).

Fee history: planned vs. actual

Despite the division's rising costs, the Town had planned to raise the yard cart fee from $75 to $100 effective July 1, 2026 — then reversed that decision, holding it at $75.

What else changed instead of the fee

Rather than raising the fee, the Town narrowed what it collects for free: loose leaf vacuuming is being phased out (avoiding the purchase of a new vacuum truck and reducing staff time), personal cans/bags are being phased out outside leaf season, and large loose piles now cost $125/truckload. Council member Melissa McCullough said eliminating leaf vacuuming "will benefit our budget because we need to buy a new vacuum truck soon, at significant taxpayer expense," and noted the labor cost of the manual work involved.

In other words: the Town appears to be managing rising Solid Waste costs primarily by reducing the scope of free service (fewer accepted container types, paid brush pickup) rather than by raising the yard cart fee itself — which is a form of cost pass-through even though the sticker price on the cart didn't move.

The evidence, side by side

You decide. Here's what points each way, based on the data above.

Evidence it's reasonable

  • The yard cart fee was held flat at $75 even as division costs rose ~4.7% — the Town absorbed cost growth rather than passing it to the fee.
  • The stated cost drivers (retirement, insurance, salaries, vehicle replacement) are routine, externally-driven inflation, not padding or service expansion.
  • Staffing levels are flat — no evidence of overstaffing or bloated headcount behind the cost growth.
  • Financial assistance, including retroactive refunds, exists for lower-income households.
  • Cutting loose-leaf vacuuming avoids a costly new vacuum truck purchase — a genuine efficiency move, not just a service cut.

Evidence it's not / you're paying more than it looks

  • Chapel Hill's $75 cart fee is about 36% higher than neighboring Carrboro's $55 fee for the same kind of cart.
  • Free service scope shrank: loose brush piles that were once part of weekly collection now cost $125/truckload, and personal cans are no longer accepted outside leaf season — real cost increases just moved off the "fee" line.
  • Some residents describe the change as landing right after a property tax increase, compounding the sense of rising costs across the board.
  • The public budget doesn't isolate yard-waste-specific cost, so there's no way to verify the $75 fee against an actual per-cart cost of service — it could be over- or under-covering the real cost.
What we don't know: the Town's budget documents don't publish a cost-per-cart or cost-per-household figure for yard waste specifically, and we don't have historical (pre-2025) yard cart fee data to see how $75 compares to, say, five years ago. Those would sharpen this comparison considerably.
